Weather Underground runs a network of community-owned personal weather stations (PWS), often just blocks from you. Readings are not professionally calibrated, so treat them as a neighborhood-level check against the official NWS data above.

Active fire perimeters within ~75 miles. Areas inside or downhill of a burn scar carry extreme flash-flood and debris-flow risk during heavy rain.

Checking active fire perimeters…

ODOT's TripCheck is Oregon's official source for road conditions, closures, chain requirements and live cameras. Its developer API isn't wired into this browser-only page, so these are direct links. In TripCheck, turn on the cameras layer and zoom to trouble spots like the I-84 Columbia Gorge, US-26 near Mt. Hood, Santiam Pass, Cabbage Hill in the Blue Mountains, and Siskiyou Summit on I-5. In Oregon you can also dial 511.

No single official statewide feed covers every Oregon utility, so pick yours for its live outage map. Rural co-ops and PUDs publish their own maps; if yours isn't listed, search its name plus “outage map”.

Published once a year, not live data — a long-range, folklore-influenced seasonal outlook, not a meteorological forecast. Useful for rough planning, not for a specific storm. Last updated in this page: October 2026, covering Winter 2026–2027.

The Old Farmer's Almanac Winter 2026–27
For western Oregon (the Almanac's Pacific Northwest region), calls for a milder-than-average but wetter winter with above-normal snowfall. The coldest snaps are expected in late December, early January, and early February; the snowiest stretches in late December, mid-January, and mid-February. Eastern Oregon falls in the Almanac's separate Intermountain region, which it also expects to be snowier than normal.  ·  Full regional forecast →
